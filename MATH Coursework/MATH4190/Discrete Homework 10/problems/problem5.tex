\section*{Problem 5}

Among any three consecutive odd integers $p,p+2,p+4$, exactly one is divisible by $3$.

\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item Find a triple of the form $p,p+2,p+4$ consisting entirely of primes.
\item Prove there is no other triple of three consecutive odd positive integers that are all prime.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Solution.}

\begin{enumerate}[(a)]
\item $\boxed{3,5,7}$.
\item For any odd $p$, the three numbers are congruent to one of $\{0,2,1\}$ modulo $3$ in some order, so one is
divisible by $3$. If all three are prime, the divisible one must equal $3$ itself; otherwise it is composite.
Hence the only possibility is the triple containing $3$, namely $3,5,7$.
\end{enumerate}
