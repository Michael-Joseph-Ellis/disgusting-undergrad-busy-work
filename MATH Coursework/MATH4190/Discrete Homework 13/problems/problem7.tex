\section*{Problem 7}

Random variables:

- \(X\): fair coin, \(\Pr(X=H)=\Pr(X=T)=\tfrac12\).
- \(Y\): biased coin, \(\Pr(Y=H)=\tfrac34\), \(\Pr(Y=T)=\tfrac14\).
- \(Z\in\{a,b,c\}\) with
  \(\Pr(Z=a)=\tfrac12,\ \Pr(Z=b)=\tfrac14,\ \Pr(Z=c)=\tfrac14\).

Entropy (in bits) is \(H(W) = -\sum p_i \log_2 p_i\).

\subsection*{(a) Entropies}

\[
H(X) = -\left(\tfrac12\log_2\tfrac12 + \tfrac12\log_2\tfrac12\right)
      = 1.
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(Y)
&= -\left(\tfrac34\log_2\tfrac34 + \tfrac14\log_2\tfrac14\right)\\
&= \tfrac34\log_2\tfrac{4}{3} + \tfrac14\cdot 2
 \approx 0.811\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\[
\begin{aligned}
H(Z)
&= -\Big(
\tfrac12\log_2\tfrac12
+ \tfrac14\log_2\tfrac14
+ \tfrac14\log_2\tfrac14
\Big)\\
&= \tfrac12\cdot 1 + \tfrac14\cdot 2 + \tfrac14\cdot 2
= 1.5\ \text{bits}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\subsection*{(b) Comparison}

Numerically,
\[
H(Z)=1.5 > H(X)=1 > H(Y)\approx 0.81.
\]

So \(Z\) is the most “uncertain” (three outcomes, fairly spread),
\(X\) is in the middle (fair coin), and \(Y\) is the least uncertain
(heavily biased toward heads). This matches the intuitive idea that the
more skewed the probabilities, the smaller the entropy.
