\section*{Problem 1. Five-card poker hand probabilities}

Let the sample space be all $5$-card hands from a $52$-card deck:
\[
\binom{52}{5}=2{,}598{,}960.
\]

\textbf{(a) Two pairs.}
Choose the two ranks for the pairs, then suits for each pair, then the rank and suit of the fifth card (not matching either pair rank).
\[
\#=\binom{13}{2}\binom{4}{2}\binom{4}{2}\cdot 11\cdot 4
=123{,}552.
\]
\[
\Pr(\text{two pairs})=\dfrac{123{,}552}{2{,}598{,}960}\approx 0.047539.
\]

\textbf{(b) Flush (not a straight flush).}
Choose a suit, choose $5$ of its $13$ cards, then subtract straight flushes in that suit.
\[
\#=4\binom{13}{5}-40=5{,}108.
\]
\[
\Pr(\text{flush})=\dfrac{5{,}108}{2{,}598{,}960}\approx 0.001965.
\]

\textbf{(c) Straight (Ace low or high, not a straight flush).}
There are $10$ possible consecutive rank sets; for each, choose any suits except the $4$ same-suit cases.
\[
\#=10\bigl(4^5-4\bigr)=10{,}200.
\]
\[
\Pr(\text{straight})=\dfrac{10{,}200}{2{,}598{,}960}\approx 0.003925.
\]

\textbf{(d) Straight flush.}
Ten rank sequences, $4$ suits each.
\[
\#=10\cdot 4=40.
\]
\[
\Pr(\text{straight flush})=\dfrac{40}{2{,}598{,}960}\approx 0.0000154.
\]

\textbf{(e) Royal flush.}
A royal flush is the highest straight flush; one per suit.
\[
\#=4.
\]
\[
\Pr(\text{royal flush})=\dfrac{4}{2{,}598{,}960}\approx 0.00000154.
\]
