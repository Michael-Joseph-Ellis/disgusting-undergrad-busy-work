\section*{Problem 7}
\textbf{Claim.} If exactly $n\ge 1$ islanders have blue eyes, then after the foreigner's announcement
(``there exists at least one blue-eyed person''), \emph{all and only} the blue-eyed islanders
commit ritual suicide, and they all do so simultaneously at noon on day $n$ (counted from the day after the announcement).

\paragraph{Why the announcement matters.}
Before the statement, everyone \emph{sees} blue eyes, but it is not common knowledge that such eyes exist.
The announcement makes ``there exists a blue-eyed person'' common knowledge and thus creates a base case
for the inductive reasoning below.

\paragraph{Proof by induction on $n$.}

\emph{Base case $n=1$.}
The unique blue-eyed islander sees no blue-eyed people. After the announcement, the only way
the statement can be true is if they themselves are blue-eyed. They therefore commit at noon on day 1.
No brown-eyed islander ever gains information that would force them to act. Hence the claim holds for $n=1$.

\emph{Inductive step.}
Assume the claim is true for $1,2,\dots,n-1$ (with $n\ge 2$).
Suppose there are exactly $n$ blue-eyed islanders.
Each blue-eyed islander sees exactly $n-1$ blue-eyed islanders.
Fix any blue-eyed islander $X$ and reason as $X$ does:

\begin{itemize}
\item If $X$ were \emph{not} blue-eyed, then there would be exactly $n-1$ blue-eyed islanders.
  By the induction hypothesis, all those $n-1$ would commit at noon on day $n-1$.
\item If noon of day $n-1$ passes and nobody has committed, that counterfactual is refuted,
  so $X$ deduces there must be $n$ blue-eyed islanders, i.e., $X$ is blue-eyed.
\end{itemize}

Because every blue-eyed islander makes the same deduction at the same time, they all commit at noon on day $n$.

Finally, any brown-eyed islander always sees $n$ blue-eyed islanders. They never encounter a refuted
counterfactual about ``$n-1$ blue-eyed'' that would force the conclusion ``I am blue-eyed'',
so they never act. This completes the induction.

\paragraph{Conclusion for the stated numbers.}
Here $n=100$, so precisely the 100 blue-eyed islanders commit at noon on day 100; the 900 brown-eyed islanders do not.
\qed
