\section*{Problem 14}

We want the launch code to be reconstructible only if
\[
3 \text{ from A},\quad 4 \text{ from B},\quad 2 \text{ from C}
\]
cooperate. Use a two–level Shamir construction.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 1: Between the three delegations.}

Let the launch code be the secret \(S\), working modulo a large prime \(p\).
Choose a random quadratic polynomial
\[
f(x)=ax^{2}+bx+S \pmod{p},
\]
so this is a \((3,3)\)-threshold scheme.

Give the three delegations the ``group shares''
\[
S_A=f(1),\quad S_B=f(2),\quad S_C=f(3).
\]
Any three of these (i.e., all of them) are needed to reconstruct \(S\).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Level 2: Inside each delegation.}

Now treat \(S_A,S_B,S_C\) as secrets and share them within each group:

\begin{itemize}
  \item For delegation A, use a \((3,10)\) Shamir scheme with secret \(S_A\).
        Each of the 10 members of A receives one share. Any 3 of them can
        recover \(S_A\), but 2 or fewer cannot.

  \item For delegation B, use a \((4,10)\) Shamir scheme with secret \(S_B\).
        Any 4 members of B together can reconstruct \(S_B\).

  \item For delegation C, use a \((2,10)\) Shamir scheme with secret \(S_C\).
        Any 2 members of C together can reconstruct \(S_C\).
\end{itemize}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Reconstruction.}

To reconstruct the launch code:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item At least 3 members of A combine their shares to obtain \(S_A\).
  \item At least 4 members of B combine theirs to obtain \(S_B\).
  \item At least 2 members of C combine theirs to obtain \(S_C\).
  \item Using the three points \((1,S_A),(2,S_B),(3,S_C)\) on \(f(x)\),
        they interpolate \(f\) and compute \(f(0)=S\), the launch code.
\end{enumerate}

Any set that is missing at least one of these group secrets cannot obtain all three
points on \(f(x)\), so they cannot reconstruct the launch code.
